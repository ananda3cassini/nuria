25-OH Vitamin D ↘

Physiology: Vitamin D is produced mainly by the skin under sunlight and, to a lesser extent, provided by food. It regulates calcium and phosphate absorption, contributes to bone strength and also plays a part in muscle function and immunity.

Decreased vitamin D: Your patient's 25-OH vitamin D level is below the reference range (62 nmol/L). This insufficiency is common at the end of winter and in people with little sun exposure. It may be associated with fatigue, muscle weakness and reduced bone mineralisation over time.

Nutritional and micronutritional advice: It is recommended to increase moderate sun exposure and the consumption of oily fish and eggs. Cu ↘ (Copper)

Physiology: Copper is a trace element required by several enzymes involved in energy production, iron transport, connective tissue formation and antioxidant protection, notably as a cofactor of superoxide dismutase.

Decreased copper: Your patient's copper level is slightly below the reference range (12.1 µmol/L, i.e. 76.9 µg/dL). A low copper status may weaken antioxidant defences and impair iron use. An excessive zinc intake should be ruled out, as zinc reduces copper absorption.

Nutritional and micronutritional advice: It is recommended to increase the consumption of copper-rich foods such as offal, shellfish, nuts and pulses. Se ↗ (Selenium)

Physiology: Selenium is a trace element built into selenoproteins such as glutathione peroxidase. It contributes to antioxidant defences, to thyroid hormone metabolism and to immune function. The margin between adequate and excessive intake is narrow.
